## 🕶️ Private tabs, from any app

Chrome, Brave, Vivaldi and Opera don't let other apps open a link in a private tab: they open an empty one at most. **Open with does it for you.** Switch on **Private** in the chooser and tap a browser, hold a browser and choose "Open in a private tab", or let a rule open a website privately every time.

Firefox and the browsers built on it take the link straight into a private tab. For Chrome-based browsers and Opera, Open with opens the new private tab and its optional accessibility service types the link in (see [Your privacy comes first](#-your-privacy-comes-first)).

Calls, site visits, clients on three messengers. Set it once and stop choosing.

## ✨ What you can do

- **Any app, not just the ones Android suggests** - Amazon links in the Amazon app, Reddit links in your favourite client, text files in the notepad you actually use.
- **Rules that open things for you** - YouTube links always in YouTube Morphe, PDFs from Gmail in Acrobat, never offer Chrome for Amazon links. Set it once, and it happens with nothing on screen. See below.
- **Rules that know the moment** - only on certain days and times, only with headphones or Bluetooth audio connected, or only in a mode you switch on, like Work, Personal or Viewing. Switch modes on General, in the chooser or from Open with's icon on your home screen, or let a mode switch itself on a schedule or with your network: on Wi-Fi, on mobile data, with no connection, while roaming or on a mobile network you name.
- **Suggestions from your own habits** - switch on Suggest rules and Open with notices when you keep picking the same app for a website, a sender or a content type, and offers the rule: one app, Always ask between the apps you use, a rule for working hours, opening without asking, or removing tracking. It counts websites by name only, on your phone.
- **Every kind of content** - links, video and audio streams, live streams (rtsp, rtmp), images, audio and video files, PDFs, ebooks, comic books, Word, Excel and PowerPoint files, text, archives, APKs, torrents, calendar files and subscriptions, contact cards, GPS tracks, map locations, navigation, email addresses, phone numbers, text messages, 2FA setup links, crypto payment links, Play Store links, web searches and shared items. Add your own file types too, each with an icon you pick (a Material icon, a picture or one from your icon pack).
- **A page for each content type** - its own default app (star it on the Apps page), "open without asking", which apps it offers and in what order, the last ones remembered (an app picked for its own links is only suggested again for links it can open), its own countdown and its own look. The chooser's Settings button takes you straight there.
- **A chooser you can make your own** - a countdown paused by any touch (its ring and seconds can be hidden separately), grid or list, apps filled from the left or the right, sizes, corners, width and position on screen, Material You colours from your wallpaper or your own, a highlight colour, outlines, fills and button shapes, and over 40 open and close animations, all with a live preview. An Always ask rule can give its chooser a look of its own.
- **Buttons your way** - Appearance lists every button in the chooser: switch each one on or off, drag it into your own order and give it your own icon (a Material icon, a picture or one from your icon pack). The same for the copy, edit and share icons beside the link.
- **Do more than open** - copy, edit, clean the tracking out of or share a link before it opens, show it as a QR code, hand it to your download manager, or send it to your read-later or notes app with **Later**. Add your own **send buttons** that hand a link to any app in one tap, with that app's icon or one you pick. **Share with** offers Open with's own list of apps to share to, and **Edit rule** opens the rule that decided the chooser. Copy a map location, phone number or email address, add a number or address to your contacts, open a place on a web map, save a copy of a file, or hand anything over to Android's own list.
- **Change links** - turn a link into another before an app is chosen: embed sites like fxtwitter.com or kkinstagram.com back to the original site, a YouTube Shorts link as a normal video, an Instagram Reel as a post, or a parameter taken out. Start from a template or write your own (replace the website, remove parameters, replace part of the path, or a regular expression), and try it on a link as you type. Each change happens by itself, or the chooser offers the original and each version as buttons. All on your phone.
- **Change the link for an app** - for apps that take a link only in their own form, such as with text added in front: the chooser offers them for their websites, and they always get the link in that form.
- **Shared links, addresses and numbers** - when something you share contains a link, **Open as link** opens it like a tapped link, and the Shares page can list apps that don't take shares; they open the link instead. A street address, phone number or email address in shared text opens the same way (in your maps, calling or email apps), and when the text holds several, the chooser lists them so you pick one. They are found on your phone, by Android's own text recognition.
- **Private tabs** - switch on **Private** in the chooser and tap a browser, or hold one and choose "Open in a private tab", or let a rule open a website privately every time. **Remember Private** keeps it on for your next links until you switch it off. Firefox and the browsers built on it take the link straight into a private tab. Chrome-based browsers (Chrome, Brave, Vivaldi...) and Opera open only an empty private tab for other apps, so Open with's optional accessibility service types the link in for you.
- **Rules in seconds** - tap **New rule** in the chooser to build one from the link in front of you, or start from one of 26 templates (work links, YouTube, meetings, a pause before your bank, PDFs from email...).
- **One app for a while** - hold any app in the chooser to use it for the next 10 minutes or the next 5 links, or to make a rule on the spot.
- **Sign-ins keep working** - Open with answers custom tab requests, so app sign-ins that need a real browser tab work with Open with as your default browser. Links that apps would show inside themselves can open in your real browser instead, with exceptions for the apps you choose, and the chooser can remember your choice per app or for all apps.
- **Help when something doesn't arrive** - "Why isn't this working?" checks your setup, names the app holding a website and shows the steps for your phone. Diagnostics shows exactly which rule would win for any link.
- **App compatibility** - see what each app can open, and why one never shows up in the chooser: switched off, only its own websites, only for file paths, or left out by a rule.
- **Safer links** - a link whose website name could pass for another (a Cyrillic "а" in pаypal.com, a capital I in place of an l) never opens by itself: the chooser warns and shows the real name. - **Android TV** - D-pad friendly throughout.
- **Backup and restore** - everything in one file you keep.
- **Share rules** - pick rules on the Rules tab and send them to another phone (or save them) as one file. **Import rules** there adds them next to the rules already on that phone, with the modes they use; a rule it already has is left out.

## 🧠 Rules

A rule decides where something opens, so you don't have to choose every time.

**What a rule can do**

- **Open with** one app of your choice, and optionally in your real browser or in the in-app view when an app asks for it, or in a browser's private tab.
- **Never offer** the apps you never want to see for that content.
- **Always ask**, bringing the chooser back for the things you want to decide each time, even where a default app would open them silently, with the app you most likely want already picked, Private already on if you like, its own countdown and its own look.
- **Also** copy the link, or save a copy of the file to a folder you pick, or **only save** it without opening anything.

**What a rule can match on**

- **The website** - by exact name, wildcard (`*.example.com`), address prefix, a word it contains, or a regular expression, with a built-in help page and tester.
- **The content type** - links, PDFs, images, map locations and the rest.
- **The file name** - starts with, ends with, contains, or a regular expression.
- **The app it came from** - only from these apps, or from any app except these.
- **When** - on certain days and at certain times (even overnight), in a mode you switch on, or with headphones or Bluetooth audio connected or not.

A rule can list several things to react to ("these websites or these files"), each with its own conditions.

**Living with them**

- **The most specific rule wins**, so a rule for one website beats a rule for all links. Between two rules for the same website, the one whose time, mode or headphones condition is met wins, so "YouTube links to YouTube Morphe" and "YouTube links to YouTube in Viewing mode" just work together.
- **Give a rule its own icon** in the Rules list, and tap **Edit rule** in the chooser to change the rule that decided it.
- **Rules name themselves** as you build them ("github.com → Firefox", "PDF documents from Gmail → Adobe Acrobat"), or you give them your own name.
- **Hold an app in the chooser** to make a rule for that website or content type without leaving what you were doing.
- **A check-up on the Rules tab** points out rules that can never do what they say: one hidden by an earlier rule with the same conditions, one whose app a Never offer rule always leaves out, one for a mode that was deleted or while modes are off, or one that can no longer save to its folder. Each says why, and a tap opens the rule in the way.
- **Share rules with another phone** - as one file; Import rules adds them, never replaces yours.

## 🔒 Your privacy comes first

- **Open with never sends your links or files anywhere.** Everything it decides, it decides on your phone. The one exception: a Google Maps short link (maps.app.goo.gl) that the app you picked can't open is looked up with Google, to find the place it points to.
- **No ads. No trackers. No analytics.**
- **No account, no sign-up, no email required.**
- **The Debug Log is optional**, off by default, and stays on your phone unless you share it.

Open with only needs to see which apps are installed (see below). Setting it as your default browser is optional and only needed for web links. If you open APK files through it, Android also asks you to allow "Install unknown apps" for Open with, because Android's installer only accepts a file from an app that is allowed to hand it over. Open with never downloads or installs anything by itself.

The **accessibility service** is optional, off until you switch it on (General > Accessibility service), and only needed for private tabs in Chrome-based browsers and Opera: when you choose a private tab, it finds that browser's address bar, types the link in, presses Enter and closes the keyboard. Until then it listens to Open with's own windows only, and to that one browser only while the link goes in. It keeps nothing and sends nothing anywhere. Firefox needs none of this.

## 🔑 Why Open with can see your installed apps

Open with asks Android for the list of installed apps. That is how it can offer **any** app, not just the ones that officially claim a link or file, and how it finds the right apps for each content type. The list is used on your phone only, to fill the chooser and the settings screens, and it never leaves your phone.

On Android 12 and newer, web links go straight to your default browser unless another app has claimed the website. So for Open with to see web links at all, you set it as your **default browser** (Settings > Apps > Default apps > Browser app). It then offers your real browsers, or opens the one your rules pick. Files, maps, email and everything else work without this.
